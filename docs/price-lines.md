# An entire coordinate-price line from one chart's minor table

Fix a stable graph basis I at a price p in one compressor epoch. For all i∈I, j∉I retain the determinant sign of Mᵢⱼ and the log proxy difference ℓᵢⱼ=f_(I−i+j)−f_I (a zero minor is recorded separately). Write tᵢⱼ(q)=ℓᵢⱼ+qᵢ−qⱼ. Stability is tᵢⱼ(p)≤1 on every nonzero entry. The same O(r(N−r)) records cover **every axis through p**, at arbitrary rational displacement. Each new query takes O(r(N−r) poly(E)) short arithmetic and zero new minor requests. Its graph basis differs from I by at most one pivot. Thus a coordinate-price search may change the physical scale by an arbitrarily large amount without exposing a new original determinant. This result is conditional on this pattern of queries; the global price learner can also make many-coordinate moves.

## Strengthen the lift before using the table

For every pair s,t in any cofactor list and each price, constrain their log difference at the lifted prices to its dyadic proxy grid cell when the proxy difference is in [−E,E], and constrain it above or below the cutoff otherwise. At y=f the requested price itself satisfies all constraints. Every condition is still a signed difference inequality, so K=O(log N) from R202 is unchanged (enlarge A by a fixed constant if needed). Perturbing g to true logs changes each difference by ≤2·2⁻ᴷ. Hence for a bounded-above log ratio t≤1, its signed physical ratio can be evaluated to absolute O(e) as sign·2^t, using zero below −E. This applies even if both entries being compared are tiny relative to some third slot. Include primal **and polar** cofactor lists, represented by complementary parent minors. The original calibration theorem stated only a comparison to the largest slot and does not by itself give this stronger pairwise statement.

## One rank-one update

Changing a pivot coordinate alters only its row of t. Changing a free coordinate alters only its column. If all t≤1, evaluate the same chart directly. Otherwise choose the largest affected t at (a,b); it exceeds one. Let m denote the true signed entry there. The inverse 1/m has bounded magnitude, and the ratio of each other nonzero affected entry to m has magnitude ≤1+O(e). For a changed row those ratios are ratios of entries of the *same primal cofactor*. For a changed column use the *same polar cofactor* (−M_:b,e_b). In particular two immense absolute entries may be compared through their bounded relative log differences without dividing two clipped values. Every unaffected entry is itself bounded and may be evaluated directly.

Exchange pivot a for free column b, retaining their positions in the two lists. Standard tableau substitution gives
```
M′_ab = 1/m
M′_al = M_al/m                      (l≠b)
M′_kb = −M_kb/m                     (k≠a)
M′_kl = M_kl − M_kb M_al/m          (k≠a,l≠b).
```
In the changed-row case evaluate M_al/m first. In the changed-column case evaluate M_kb/m first. Every factor now computed has magnitude at most three; no positive huge power is taken. All true new entries have magnitude <7. If each bounded primitive is within 16e, replacing the factors in the subtraction gives error ≤16e(1+3+2)+256e²<100e at E≥12. Using the uniform conservative bound 256e covers a final rounding too. The direct capped graph proof with d=256e still satisfies 6N²d<τ/2 for E=⌈log₂(10⁷(N+1)³)⌉. At most one swap occurs in this construction. Uniform translation of all prices is free because every fundamental log uses a price difference and the relevant LP circuits are monochromatic. The same result therefore covers price vectors whose difference from p is constant except at one coordinate.

The common lift can differ between different points on a line. The certificate proof applies to each price with its own valid lift. All actual inputs to the line evaluator already came from the epoch's common proxy table. Any later extension consistent with those stored answers works for every virtual query made from them. Flush tables when the source compressor restarts. A new stable base is needed before repeating the argument around a pivoted output, since one pivot is enough to produce a bounded chart but need not make the new center 2-stable.

## A uniformly accurate short surrogate along an axis

The replacement index is fixed on the entire line: all affected log scores change by the same amount. Let b be that winner and u the largest affected score at the query. Center a surrogate at the price where u=0. Store the unaffected graph entries and the affected row (or column) divided by its winner's absolute value, using signed bounded dyadics from the table. Its winner coefficient is exactly ±1. If the affected entries all vanish, this coordinate is a loop or coloop and every price gives the same graph. Otherwise all entries at the neutral price are bounded by three and have O(log N) bits. At a new price the formal surrogate scales just that row or column by 2^u. For u≤1 use the original chart; its affected entries are a bounded factor times the cached ratios. For u>1 use the pivoted chart: all entries away from the pivot row and column are fixed differences of bounded products, and the inverse-winner row or column scales only by 2^(−u). Both representations stay uniformly close, entrywise O(e), to valid physical charts at their respective prices by the strengthened common-lift argument. A constant-factor increase in E accommodates the products. One scalar bounded exponentiation per query suffices; its huge opposite power is never computed. This is a short rational surrogate *for a price line*, with no requirement that the true lifted price differences equal the nominal price differences globally.

For numerical application set D_d=I+(d−1)vvᵀ, where v is the coordinate unit vector. If P projects onto the neutral surrogate, w=Pv and s=vᵀPv, the Sherman–Morrison identity gives the updated projector

    P_d = D_d [P−((d²−1)/(1+(d²−1)s))wwᵀ] D_d.

Use d=2^u for a free-column scaling and d=2^(−u) for a pivot-row scaling. A stable description splits off the two-dimensional plane of v and w. For 0<s<1, write a=(w−sv)/√(s(1−s)), and v₀=√s v+√(1−s)a. Then P_d=P−v₀v₀ᵀ+v_d v_dᵀ, where v_d=√s_d v+√(1−s_d)a and s_d=d²s/(1−s+d²s). Only bounded logistic weights enter; use d or its inverse according to which is ≤1. The neutral chart has a unit pivot and a unit exchange at this coordinate, so s and 1−s are each ≥1/(1+9N²): a unit witness in each corresponding graph containing that slot follows by normalizing its graph basis row. This gives polynomial sensitivity when forming the two-dimensional plane. Loops and coloops are constant. Thus an axis can also reuse its projector with two rank-one terms per query. This identity is classical matrix algebra; the uniform short surrogate is the calibration step that makes it usable across arbitrarily distant compressed prices.
