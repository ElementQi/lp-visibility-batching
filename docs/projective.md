# Removing positive coordinate gauges before pricing

Choose a column order and any fixed independent coordinate basis by ordinary elimination. Write the original row space in that basis as $[I\mid M]$. Its bipartite support graph joins pivot $i$ to free coordinate $j$ whenever $M_{ij}\ne0$. Let $q=|E|-N+c$ where $c$ counts all graph components, including isolated vertices. This is the number of off-tree edges in a spanning forest. Choose a forest by label order. One traversal supplies positive rational factors $s$ with $|M_{ij}|s_j/s_i=1$ on its edges, by fixing the root factor to one. All original rows and polar vectors can be recovered by reciprocal positive scaling. Exact products on tree paths have polynomial height. Forest normalization is classical. Here it canonicalizes the actual input before the general price learner and controls the number of genuinely variable magnitudes. [Pendavingh and van Zwam, normalization of representations](https://arxiv.org/abs/0806.4487)

The signed normalized matrix is identical under every positive diagonal scaling and every invertible change of row coordinates, for the fixed order and basis rule. Indeed the new forest factors and the old ones times the inverse input scales differ only by a common constant on each connected component. The only variable data in the normalized chart are the $q$ chord magnitudes. They are alternating products and quotients around fundamental cycles. If any projectively equivalent rational input has entry height at most $L$, elimination on that input gives chart entries of height polynomial in $L,N$. The forest transformation multiplies at most $N$ such ratios. Since the normalized charts are identical, its height is bounded polynomially by $N$ and the best height in this positive projective equivalence class. Normalization can magnify some individual entries; the ordinary compressed graph construction remains applicable.

## Uniform compressor dependence on cycle rank

Every normalized maximal minor is, up to sign, a square subdeterminant of the free block. Expand by which chord entries appear. Each chosen subset of chords must be a matching. Removing the selected rows and columns leaves only forest edges, which have at most one perfect matching (two distinct perfect matchings would give an alternating cycle). Thus each squarefree chord monomial appears with coefficient in $\{-1,0,1\}$. The maximum degree in any one chord is one and the coefficient one-norm is at most $2^q$. The same polynomial family covers every original maximal minor, regardless of $N$.

For $D$ approximately dissociated minor logs, the upstream subset-product counting argument gives $2^{2^D}$ distinct integer polynomials evaluated at the chord magnitudes. Each variable has degree at most $D$, giving at most $(D+1)^q$ monomials; coefficients have magnitude at most $2^{D(q+1)}$. Taking two logarithms yields the necessary inequality

$$D\le q\log_2(D+1)+\log_2(2+D(q+1)).$$

Taking $D=64(q+1)\lceil\log_2(4(q+2))\rceil$ violates it. For example with $H=\lceil\log_2(4(q+2))\rceil\ge3$, $\log_2(D+1)\le7+2H$ and $\log_2(2+D(q+1))\le9+3H$. Thus a general implementation can replace the uniform dissociation cap by $O((q+1)\log(q+2))$. Combined with a refreshed calibrated graph and the [affine extension bound](extension-height.md), its supplied demand range obeys

$$\log R=O\big((q+1)^3\log^3(q+2)[\log(N+1)+\log(q+2)]\big).$$

It uses the same paid compressor operations and guarantees in every dimension. Even when one block is disconnected, count all components and zeros as above. At $q=0$ all nonzero maximal minors have magnitude one. An exact short LP suffices immediately.

## Every rational two-chord target has a constant-height surrogate

When $q=1$, replace the positive chord magnitude by $1/2,1,2$ according to its position relative to one. For $q=2$ all nonzero determinant polynomials up to an overall sign belong to the forty possible forms $a+bx+cy+dxy$ with coefficients in $\{-1,0,1\}$ and first nonzero coefficient positive. On the positive rational quadrant there are exactly 201 sign profiles for these forty forms, counting equality faces. Every such profile has a representative with all positive numerators and denominators at most **eight**. Substituting the corresponding chord magnitudes into the normalized chart preserves the signs of every maximal minor, including zeros. Signs of the chords themselves stay fixed. This gives the entire represented oriented matroid on arbitrarily many elements with a four-bit free block.

Here is the finite completeness proof. The vertical order of the sections $y=-(a+bx)/(c+dx)$ can change only at real roots of their pairwise cross products, at poles or at crossings of zero. Include the roots of all numerators and denominators, and zero. The resulting polynomials have degree at most two. Isolate their distinct positive real roots with rational disjoint brackets: integer square root gives directed endpoints for each quadratic radical, and equal irrational roots belong to the same primitive irreducible quadratic. For each gap between successive brackets take an interior rational sample; include all rational critical endpoints and one sample after the last critical root. Rational original $x$ cannot equal an irrational critical endpoint. At each sampled $x$ include every positive rational section and a sample in every adjacent positive interval. Vertical identically-zero forms, coincidences, and constant forms are therefore included. On a noncritical open $x$ interval both the order and signs between or on these sections are fixed. This covers every positive rational pair. The integer checker generates 19 horizontal samples, enumerates the profiles exactly, then compares them to the signs on the grid with numerators and denominators at most eight. Smaller square caps are also checked in order. [Exact arrangement and grid certificate](../bench/check_chord_profiles.py)

Solve a target on the short primal/polar pair using an ordinary bounded-bit LP and extract an elementary nonnegative vector with positive target. Its zero columns contain a basis $J$ of rank one below the chosen side's rank. On the original side the row obtained by vanishing on $J$ and fixing the target to one exists and has the same signs, by the equality of the oriented maximal minors. Complementary minors prove the polar case. Thus a single short LP and original elimination give an actual original target certificate without price searches. Exact original checks on 75 arbitrary gauge-distorted rational matrices of all three excesses compared 8,085 maximal minors and recovered all 633 target circuits. [Public normalization and lift routines](../visibility/projective.py). The reference simplex is an exact experiment. The dimension-only complexity proof uses a polynomial-time LP algorithm on the short instance.
