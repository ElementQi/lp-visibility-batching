# A rational realization of a short graph and transport back to the original LP

Let $[I\mid M^0]$ be the original rational positive chart on the same ordered pivot/free indices as a returned rational approximation $[I\mid B]$ of entry error $d$. One common *real* diagonal $D_\star$ is known to make the true graph $d$-close to $B$. The numerical values of this diagonal may be unavailable. In $O(Nr(N-r))$ rational multiplications/comparisons, one can construct a positive rational diagonal $Q$ for the original source such that

$$M^Q_{ij}=M^0_{ij}Q_j/Q_i,\qquad |M^Q_{ij}-B_{ij}|\le d$$

at every entry. All intermediate rational heights are polynomial in the original input and returned chart sizes. The certificate is the list of $N$ diagonal entries, verified by the displayed inequalities. It imposes the recorded graph-entry intervals. The unqueried circuit-demand comparisons can still refer to the external common lift.

For $M^0_{ij}\ne0$ put $b=\operatorname{sgn}(M^0_{ij})B_{ij}$, $l=\max(0,b-d)$ and $u=b+d$. Require $Q_j\le uQ_i/|M^0_{ij}|$ and, when $l>0$, $Q_i\le |M^0_{ij}|Q_j/l$. A nonzero minor requires $u>0$. A zero minor needs $|B_{ij}|\le d$. In the directed graph of upper bounds $Q_v\le c_{uv}Q_u$, each edge multiplier is positive rational. A known feasible real diagonal implies that every directed cycle has product at least one. Add a supersource of unit-length products to every node. Set $Q_v$ to the least product along a path to $v$. Deleting cycles can only decrease products, so a simple path attains each minimum. The resulting positive potentials satisfy every bound. Synchronous Bellman–Ford multiplication and one final edge check compute them with path products of length at most $N$. Inconsistent inputs can be rejected without allowing intermediate bit lengths to grow around a bad cycle. Rank-zero and full-rank cases have empty edge sets. Exact zeros, enormous ratios, disconnected components, and one-sided intervals at clipped entries require no new primitive. Ordinary elimination produces $M^0$ on the selected pivots at polynomial rational height. The signed-coordinate polar graph follows identically from transposition and reciprocal scaling.

## Uniform transport of strict positive solutions

For any short nonnegative coefficient row $a$ with $\|a\|_1\le H$, every true nonpivot component of $a[I\mid M^Q]$ is within $Hd$ of the corresponding component of $a[I\mid B]$. Thus a candidate with each required nonpivot component at least $Hd$ transports to a nonnegative original vector by evaluating at $M^Q$ and dividing its components by $Q$. A positive target is ensured by a strict target inequality or positive target pivot. For the polar chart use $[-(M^Q)^\top\mid I]$ and multiply by $Q$ after evaluation. Exact checks can accept even when some short entries fail the sufficient margin test. For a prescribed support, perform the operation on that *restricted* problem, then extend by zeros. Exact off-support zeros cannot be inferred from mere small magnitude in an unrestricted graph.

In particular, suppose at a price on a target-compatible restricted problem every surviving coordinate has some positive-side nonnegative witness, sup-normalized, of head at least $\theta$. Under the common lift, sum one such witness per head and divide by the number $m$ of surviving coordinates. The shared true vector lies in $[0,1]^m$ with all components $\ge\theta/m$. Its pivot coefficients are in $[0,1]$. In the short chart the same coefficients produce every component at least $\theta/m-md$. If $2md<\theta/m$, a short LP with $0\le a\le1$ finds a vector with entries strictly above $md$ (maximize the minimum if $\theta$ is unknown). Original-space recovery follows from the computed rational $Q$. Only the short LP depends on a bit-polynomial numerical feasibility subroutine. The postprocessing is strongly polynomial arithmetic on the original rationals. The same argument works whenever one common-lift witness has sufficient minimum positive component. A single visible head permits arbitrarily tiny other positive components and does not by itself guarantee this premise.

Earlier original-point recovery used a physical price zero or relied on verifying an exact support guess. The bounded graph now realizes a physical diagonal at *any successful compressed price*, and transports robust vectors through it. Finding an interior price or a correct target-compatible support at general input cost remains a distinct search problem. The construction also handles several known charts or prices at once if their edge multipliers have been converted to one common set of diagonal unknowns and a shared feasible solution exists. Generic calibrated queries may have unrelated hidden diagonals.
