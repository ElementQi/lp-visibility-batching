# Low-precision calibration for LP pricing through a complementary graph

## Interface change

In OpenAI's paired instance W=C×C⊥ order the rows of the positive block first and give the online minor compressor the usual entire W. At a completed price query we seek just a positive graph on pivot slots I, with its complementary graph recovered by transposition. This interface is sufficient for LP witness pricing on every restriction of C. The ordered, lower-triangular orientation guarantee used in the separate quadratic-programming application is outside this argument. Denote the rank of C by r. At r=0 or r=N all sides follow immediately. One may order the smaller-rank side first and interchange slot signs in what follows.

## One common lift pays for every exchange

Use the published compression and common-lift construction with cutoff E, e=2⁻ᴱ, A=(E+5)2ᴱ, and K the least integer with 2ᴷ≥(N+1)A^(N+1). Request minor proxies lazily. If a call restarts, discard the partial chart and restart search as the published algorithm does. For any completed finite transcript there is a common proxy extension f in that epoch and, for this price, a single effective diagonal D. The common-lift inequalities concern **every cofactor list simultaneously**. For every row with proxy-maximizing slot t and supported s, the true ratio |(cD)ₛ/(cD)ₜ| differs from 2^(ρₛ(f,p)−ρₜ(f,p)) by at most 8e. The circuit-demand argument of the published paper uses the same f and D and gives visibility at every surplus maximizer. No step of chart search needs the effective diagonal explicitly. [Calibration, common lift and pivot estimate](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/calibration.tex)

1. Grow an initial positive basis I by the published prefix cofactor pivot rule, stopping at row r. Only positive columns can have nonzero entries. Normalize the r true elimination rows by their respective pivots. The matrix on I is upper triangular with unit diagonal, and every entry of the full normalized rows has magnitude at most 1+8e<2. Therefore the ratio between the largest scaled r-minor and the initial scaled minor is ≤(2√r)^r by Hadamard. This argument is uniform over all consistent common lifts.
2. For each i∈I, take the full positive-prefix cofactor list with T=I without i, in a fixed order. Its supported entries are single exchanges and the existing pivot i. Choose a proxy-maximizing t in this row. If ρₜ−ρᵢ>1, replace i by t and restart the scan. This comparison uses only linear forms in short proxies and prices. The true old-to-new volume ratio is |(cD)ᵢ/(cD)ₜ|≤1/2+8e≤2/3 when e≤1/64. Thus every exchange grows the same true scaled minor volume by ≥3/2. For any finite prefix of an unfinished query one may extend its f and choose its D to bound the number of exchanges in that prefix. Although future replies can change which extension witnesses past results, the numerical bound is uniform over all extensions. There are at most r log(2√r)/log(3/2) exchanges per attempt, including attempts cut off by a restart.
3. At termination the proxy ratio at i is at least 1/2 of the row maximum. The true ratio is at least 1/2−8e. Every true entry of the fundamental chart is at most (1+8e)/(1/2−8e)≤3 for e≤1/128. To construct the entry in column j, divide the signed proxy-normalized ratio for j by the positive proxy-normalized ratio for i and use the true determinant signs. Approximate each normalized ratio to absolute error ≤e, using zero for a sufficiently negative exponent, as in the published routine. Each numerator and denominator then differs from its true normalized value by ≤9e, and the denominator is ≥3/8 for e≤1/128. For example the quotient error is at most 9e(1+3)/(3/8)=96e (the identity pivot columns may be set exactly). This constructs M approximating a genuine positive graph [I | M★]. Because scaling is reciprocal, the true opposite graph in the same slot order is exactly [−M★ᵀ | I]. All witness coefficients in either chart are the entries at its own pivot slots; their sup norm is ≤1 for every sup-norm-one vector. We recover this property even when the initial triangular basis has exponentially bad growth.

There are O(rN) proxy lookups per complete scan, O(1+r log(r+1)) scans and O(rN) initial prefix lookups. Each candidate is an actual minor of the matrix given to the published online compressor. Exact signs and zero tests use original minor arithmetic. Previously queried minors can be cached within the epoch. This is a dimension-polynomial search, with more worst-case requests per completed price than the original triangular pass. Choosing the smaller side gives r≤N/2 and a chart with r(N−r) stored entries.

## Direct all-head test at logarithmic precision

Take the returned approximate graph with entry error d≤96e. The positive variable is a row coefficient vector a∈[0,1]^r and u=(a,aM); impose −Nd≤uⱼ≤1+Nd on all slots, then shift x=u+Nd·1≥0. If v≥0 is a true normalized positive witness, setting a=v_I puts u within Nd of v and makes x≥v. A true polar witness w∈[0,1]^N is orthogonal to (a,aM★). Therefore x·w≤2N²d for every feasible a, and a polar-visible head w_i≥1/3 implies x_i≤6N²d. Choose τ=1/(32N) and E=⌈log₂(10⁷(N+1)³)⌉, which ensures 6N²d<τ/2. On the same polytope maximize ∑t with 0≤t≤min(x,τ). Any positive visible head has a feasible competitor with x_i≥1/3 and x≥0. The cap-deficit proof applies because all demand slots keep the same nonnegative shift and all coefficients lie in one convex feasible box. A sufficiently accurate optimizer (e.g., gap τ/32) has t_i>τ/2 for positive-visible heads. Polar-visible heads have t_i≤x_i<τ/2. This test directly consumes a sparse graph and needs neither a projector nor the coefficient budget H. Approximate exponentiation and the final chart can be gridded dyadically to absolute O(e) error by increasing E by a constant.

The LP-specific cutoff is therefore E=O(log(N+1)); the explicit K in the published common lift becomes O(N log(N+1)). In the published label parameters 2⁻ᴾ=(2^(100N²))⁻¹⁰⁰ gives P=10000N² and K=Θ(N³). Substituting their actual triangular coefficient bound 3^(r−1) already permits an intermediate O(N+log N) precision budget for the projection test. The complementary chart removes the remaining exponential dependence on triangular growth. Reuse the *entire* external online compression theorem and pricing/restart/recovery rules. The reduction changes one of the parameters that drives those procedures. Comparing total running times requires paying for the extra minor lookups, and reading the dependence of the external compressor and price learner on K and R. This result applies to every LP input and every restriction used in witness recovery. The scheduled probe count is derived below.

## Provenance

The determinant exchanges use the classical barycentric-spanner principle and are closely related to strong rank-revealing QR. [Awerbuch and Kleinberg](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~rdk/papers/OLSP.pdf) · [Gu and Eisenstat](https://doi.org/10.1137/0917055) The specific obligations here are that proxy-guided swaps ascend under one unknown common lift, the opposing scaled chart is recovered without querying its minors, the witness coefficients are exactly unit-bounded, and the reduced compression precision suffices for every price-label obligation. For the upstream sources, see [LP pricing and original parameter schedule](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/lp.tex).

## The common lift only needs signed difference constraints

There is a second, independent improvement that retains even the published ordered basis. Each common-lift inequality is an upper bound on σpᵢ−τpⱼ, where σ,τ∈{−1,+1}. The right side c(y) has two minor-log terms and a grid constant in [−E−e,E+e]. Reversing a lower bound gives the same form. Create vertices (i,+),(i,−). For σpᵢ−τpⱼ≤c add the edge (j,τ)→(i,σ) and the reflected edge (i,−σ)→(j,−τ), both of cost c. This includes unary 2pᵢ bounds and self-loops. Feasibility of the actual price inequalities is equivalent to absence of a negative directed cycle. Indeed signed prices give feasible potentials on both edges. Conversely take any feasible difference potentials v and antisymmetrize pᵢ=(vᵢ₊−vᵢ₋)/2. Averaging the two edge inequalities gives exactly the original bound. Potentials exist for any finite graph without negative cycles by shortest paths from a zero-cost supersource.

At y=f the prices used on the query witness feasibility. Every simple directed cycle uses at most 2N edges. Multiplying each of its costs by 2ᴱ yields integral affine forms in minor parameters with coefficient 1-norm at most A=(E+5)2ᴱ each. Thus if f∼₂ᴷg and 2ᴷ≥2NA, every cycle has nonnegative cost also at g. This proves the common-lift existence step with

    K=⌈log₂(2N(E+5)2ᴱ)⌉ = E + O(log(N+1)+log(E+1)).

The later passage from g to physical log magnitudes ℓ still requires 2⁻ᴷ≤e. The displayed choice guarantees it. All rows are weak inequalities, including the tail cutoff. Zero-cost cycles, tied maxima, both signs of a coordinate, and repeated parameters are harmless. The doubling is the standard octagonal difference-bound construction. Its application here bounds the affine type radius of the compressor. [Miné](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10990-006-8609-1)

The graph is an existence proof across all cofactor lists, which can be exponentially numerous. The implementation continues to query only needed minors and never constructs this graph. This improvement needs no pivot exchanges, modifies only the type radius sent to the external compressor, and also works for mixed signed supports and the ordered calibration theorem.

Combining the graph chart with the cycle bound gives **K=O(log(N+1)) for general LP price calibration** at one-third visibility. The original explicit K=Θ(N³) uses the general Farkas projection estimate. Keeping the original P and replacing only the common-lift lemma gives K=Θ(N²), with no extra per-query minor requests. The interface changes and their cost should be stated separately. The generic online compressor itself has a second extension step with many log variables and a different coefficient structure. The signed-cycle argument here does not automatically simplify that step.

## An explicit whole-learner probe improvement

Fix the published choices throughout the online compressor, including its generous dissociation cap D=(2N+1)^6, the upper bound U=B^(p+2)/η and the largest extension bound over 0≤p≤m<D. Write L=K+log(D+1). Its actual parameter schedule has

    log(1/η)=Θ(L),   log M=Θ(D L),
    max log C=Θ(D² L),  max log B=Θ(D² L),
    max log U=Θ(D³ L),  log F=Θ(D⁴ L).

The upper estimates follow by substituting A=2ᴷ(D+4)/η, M=(D+1)A^(D+1), ε⁻¹=100(m+1)M, C=2^(m(m+1))(2/ε)^(m+1), H=100MC, and B=10M(mH+3). The last extension bound is (D+2)(AU+1)^(D+1)U/η. Matching orders follow by taking the prescribed allowed term m=p=D−1 in the uniform maximum. This measures the uniform bound fed to the algorithm, including terms the current input might never need. It makes no lower-bound assertion about possible ranges of actual demands. [Compression schedule](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/compression.tex)

The paper uses R=4F, nonroot branching b=2¹⁹+1, depth h the least positive odd integer with (5/4)^h>2N and r₀=b+3072⌈log₂(|E_market|+1)⌉. A demand guess ranges over [−R,R], and expert choices have polynomial cardinality in N,R, so log |E_market|=Θ(log(R+1)+log(N+1)) for these ranges. The number of leaf oracle calls in a completed full fixed-schedule run is exactly r₀ b^(h−1) (the same depth and branching in each comparison). The published algorithm also queries at prices returned by internal children. Counting every child return gives exactly r₀(1+b+⋯+b^(h−1)) calls in a completed tree without caching. Thus changing K from Θ(N³) to O(log N), keeping D and the rest of the published recipes fixed, reduces the prescribed per-completed-run probe count by a factor **Θ(N³/log(N+1))**. Even the cycle-only replacement, which retains every original lookup strategy and precision, saves a factor Θ(N) on this schedule. [Price tree and exact root budget](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/build/prices.tex)

The chart search charges O(N³ log(N+1)) minor probes per price in the worst case, versus O(N²) in the old scan. Charging both schedules at these per-call worst-case rates still improves the resulting **upper bound** on total minor-proxy lookups by order N²/log²(N+1). At most D−1 aborted epochs and at most N+1 side decisions to reconstruct a target circuit supply common worst-case multiplicative caps (using the dimension of each call if it shrinks). Actual restart locations, early successes, per-request rational arithmetic, price-vertex optimization and overall wall time can differ. The proved improvement is in the scheduled-call count and the charged lookup bound. It pays for the new exchanges and reaches beyond replacing the label solver at a supplied price.

## Why some logarithmic chart precision is necessary

Let d≥2 be even, N=d+2, and the positive graph be [I₂ | M]. Split the d columns into equal groups. In the first group the column of M is (−1/d,h/d), and in the second it is (h/d,−1/d). Consider h=5/4 and h=1/4. Both charts have the same entry signs, the same pivots and entries bounded by one. With h=5/4, a=(1,1) gives a nonnegative graph vector with first head one and all nonpivot coordinates 1/(4d). With h=1/4, b=1 gives the polar graph vector (−Mb,b), whose first two heads both equal 3/8 and whose maximum coordinate is one. The first head has opposite mandatory signs in the two cases. The midpoint rounded chart is compatible with both at absolute entry error 1/(2d). Hence any decoder supplied only such a chart, its entry signs and a uniform error radius needs Ω(log N) binary places in the worst case to resolve visible labels. This matches the order of sufficiency for the chart-only interface. Additional original minor comparisons can distinguish the examples. This statement concerns the representation, independent of algorithm running time.

## An exponential precision separation for the ordered triangular array

Set N=2m with m≥5 and a=b=(1,1/2,…,2^(−(m−2)),2^(−(m−2)))∈ℝᵐ. Thus each aᵢ before the last equals the sum of its successors. Let U be unit upper triangular with every entry strictly above the diagonal equal to −1. Then Ua=aₘeₘ and Ub=bₘeₘ. Define two true complementary graph pairs from

    M⁺ = M₀ + abᵀ,           M⁻ = M₀ − abᵀ,
    (M₀)ᵢⱼ = 2aᵢbₘ if j=m,
             −2aₘbⱼ if i=m and j<m,
             0 otherwise.

Supply a paired triangular array by taking U[I M] on the positive slots and U[−Mᵀ I] on the negative slots, with zeros in the other blocks. In each block the own pivot columns form U. Positive pivot order is the initial identity block, and on the polar side its own pivots can be visited first when breaking maximum ties (equivalently arrange the slot order, or use that all the nonpivot entries here are strictly below one for m≥5). Rows vanish at earlier pivots, the current pivot is one, and all entries have absolute value at most one for m≥5. For example for j<m the last primal row is (−2±1)aₘbⱼ, while its last entry is (2±1)aₘbₘ. For i<m the changed polar row has entries −(2±1)aᵢbₘ and at i=m the same formula applies. All other entries coincide because Ua and Ub have only one nonzero coordinate. The signs of every entry of both supplied paired arrays are identical, including all zeros. Every true normalized row-coefficient bound from the triangular calibration is satisfied.

Nevertheless with M⁺ the positive graph vector selected by the first pivot has first head one, nonnegative nonpivot entries bⱼ for j<m and 3bₘ at j=m, and infinity norm one. With M⁻ the polar graph vector selected by the first free coordinate has first pivot head one, nonnegative pivot entries aᵢ for i<m and 3aₘ at i=m, and infinity norm one. The two inputs demand opposite signs at the first head. The two triangular arrays differ in sup norm by 2aₘ=2^(3−m), and their midpoint is compatible with each at error aₘ. Hence uniform resolution of this representation needs Ω(N) bits of absolute entry precision, even given all exact represented entry signs and the pivot indices. These arrays are legitimate ordered elimination rows at zero price: take the displayed triangular arrays as ordered input rows; each new elimination cofactor is a positive multiple of the displayed row, with a unit maximizing own pivot. The example is a representation statement; additional access to original minor signs can distinguish the sources.

Conversely, for an ordered unit-pivot triangular basis with entries at most two, elimination gives sup-norm coefficient bound at most 3^(N−1) in the paired LP of rank N. In the earlier approximate-projector estimate the subspace error is O(N^(3/2)3^N 2^(−P)). Choosing P=O(N+log(N+1)) makes it inverse-polynomial, sufficient for the proven visible-label test. Thus the worst-case precision of the ordered-array interface is Θ(N), whereas the bounded graph interface has Θ(log(N+1)) upper and lower bounds. The original published numerical choice is even more conservative. Comparing against the tightened triangular baseline with the same signed-cycle lift gives a factor of order N/log N in the prescribed completed-tree probe budget. Extra chart lookups still require a separate comparison against that stronger baseline.

## Share one minor compressor across the entire witness recovery

Let A represent the original r-dimensional positive row space. Only proxies of its full r-row minors are needed. To see this first choose any pivot basis I₀=(i₁,…,iᵣ) and take the graph gauge A_I₀⁻¹A. Its first k rows on a k-list T have determinant, up to the ordering sign,

    det A[:, (T, iₖ₊₁,…,iᵣ)] / det A[:, I₀].

If a column repeats then the determinant is zero. This is expansion along the remaining identity columns after basis change. The common denominator cancels in a normalized cofactor list, in every log-score difference and in the pivot-normalized signs. Thus the greedy initialization as well as the exchange scans can use the full parent minor indexed by the completion. For dual circuits use complementary maximal minors of A: for any ordered basis of ker A their absolute determinants are a common positive constant times the absolute complementary r-minors of A. This follows directly in graph coordinates [I M], [−Mᵀ I] by expanding both determinants on identity columns. All signs follow from the required permutations. The signs in one circuit may also all be reversed without affecting its row normalization.

Now impose v_z=0 for a set Z on a chosen side. In that side's column representation choose an independent basis J of the columns in Z. The remaining subspace on unpinned coordinates is represented by contracting J and deleting the other pinned columns (loops after contraction). Its maximal minor on T is a common nonzero scalar times the previous maximal minor on (J,T). Indeed choose a basis of the columns starting with J and put those columns in identity form; expand the larger determinant along J. Prefix minors of the child again complete to a maximal minor by its own fixed pivot basis. If the chosen side was the original polar, follow these operations by the complement map back to A. Rank-zero and full-rank children have immediate decisions. Every nontrivial normalized cofactor list of every restricted problem therefore takes its absolute logarithms, up to a common within-list constant, from the *same original full-minor index set*. Original determinant signs and ranks remain available by ordinary elimination. Loops, coloops, and repeated columns in a completion give precisely the zeros excluded from the list.

Use one external online compressor on A throughout target recovery. At any successful child price query, build the signed difference system for that child's *entire* cofactor family, with y-coordinates the referenced original minor logs. Repeated indices cancel or sum normally. The common-denominator constants of the minor representation cancel from differences. A proxy extension f and comparison g for the parent indices therefore give the child's common lift by the same ≤2N cycle argument; the perturbation bound on differences is still 2·2⁻ᴷ. Demands may be defined by the fixed representative's parent proxy at each slot, plus the usual shift and floor. They are fixed for each active child search throughout an epoch. The hidden effective diagonal need only exist on that child's current coordinates. There is no requirement that the lifted diagonals of successive children agree.

If a proxy request restarts, discard the current incomplete price run and chart, and retain the parent's compressor state. Previously completed side decisions and kept restrictions are exact facts about the original subspace. They remain valid after a restart. If there are s completed side decisions (at most N+1 for the paper's target recovery), there are at most s+D−1 price-run attempts in total, because each interrupted run consumes one *global* restart and the parent compressor can restart at most D−1 times. This replaces sD in a cold-restart worst-case ledger. With one input A the compressor's ambient column parameter is N rather than 2N. The new global accounting applies equally to output-sensitive deletion. Its proof relies on lifting each minor index to the fixed original source; sharing the cache of numerical answers across unrelated calibrators would not justify it.
